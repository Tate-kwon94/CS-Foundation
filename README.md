# CS Foundations — Energy Engineer → Software Engineer

> Building a computer science foundation while moving from nuclear power engineering
> toward software engineering and Industrial AI.

This repository documents a long-term, self-directed study of computer science
fundamentals — with an emphasis on understanding concepts deeply and implementing
them from scratch rather than collecting certificates.

## Background

I work as an engineer in the nuclear power industry — four years operating plant
control systems in a main control room, then technical management and construction
supervision on a large international NPP project. Along the way I built AI/OCR-based
document-verification and automation tools for my own team, and hit a ceiling: I could
assemble solutions, but I lacked the fundamentals underneath them. This repository is
how I close that gap.

My long-term focus is the intersection of software, AI, and complex engineering systems.

## Ground rules

1. **Fundamentals are written without AI assistance.** In `python-basics/`,
   `data-structures/`, and `leetcode/`, every line is hand-written; AI is used only
   after solving, for review. The point of this repo is to build what AI can't give
   me — the ability to reason about code on my own.
2. **Every study session ends with a commit.** Small is fine.
3. **No coursework code.** Assignments and challenge problems from courses I take are
   never committed here — that would violate course honor policies. What lives here is
   my own writing: concept notes in my own words, and re-implementations built from
   scratch on different examples.
4. **No confidential material.** Nothing from my employer or its projects — no internal
   documents, drawings, data, contract values, or design specifics. Domain-inspired
   projects use synthetic data and generalized problem statements only.
5. **AI-assisted review material is labeled and separated.** Files under `review/`
   contain generated questions, runnable puzzles, and answer keys used for practice.
   They are study tools, not evidence of independent implementation. Portfolio work
   in the fundamentals directories remains hand-written under rule 1.
