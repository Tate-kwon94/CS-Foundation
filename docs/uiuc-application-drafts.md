## 1. Academic Statement of Purpose (초안, 약 650단어 ≈ 1.5쪽)

> For four years, I operated the control systems of a 1,000 MW nuclear power plant from its main control room. My work was a continuous dialogue with logic: interlock chains that decide whether a pump may start, automatic control sequences that hold a reactor's electrical systems within tight bounds, alarm cascades that demand a correct diagnosis in minutes. I did not write that logic — I operated it, tested its edges, and learned to reason about a complex system through its signals and states. The Master of Computer Science at Illinois is how I move to the other side of that dialogue: from operating engineered logic to designing and building it.
>
> My path into control rooms ran through a deliberate detour. As an undergraduate in Plant System Engineering at Kyungpook National University, I extended my enrollment to complete a minor in Electrical Engineering — accepting a delayed graduation and, during those final semesters, weaker grades, as I carried cross-disciplinary coursework while securing my first engineering position. That tradeoff defined my career. Immediately after graduation, the minor became my profession: I joined Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power as a power systems operator, working across voltage levels from 765 kV transmission to 480 V plant distribution. Among roughly 8,000 employees of the nuclear division, I ranked first in performance-improvement activities and received the plant's top operator award. Since graduation I have also passed two of the most demanding professional examinations in my field — the U.S. Professional Engineer exam (Mechanical: Thermal and Fluid Systems) and the PMP — evidence, I believe, of how I study when the outcome matters.
>
> In 2024 I moved to the El-Dabaa Nuclear Power Plant project in Egypt as a technical lead on the owner's engineering side, and it was here that I crossed from operating systems to building software. Confronted with thousands of contract documents, drawings, and inspection certificates that were being sampled rather than fully verified, I built an AI/OCR-based verification pipeline that converted manual spot-checking into data-driven full verification. I developed a program that automatically pre-screens nuclear fuel transport windows using weather data, and an automated consistency checker between new drawings and technical codes. These tools work — but building them showed me precisely where my ceiling is. I can assemble solutions with modern AI tooling; what I lack is the computer science underneath: the data structures, algorithms, and systems thinking that separate an engineer who scripts from an engineer who designs.
>
> I have spent this year closing that gap deliberately. I completed the University of Illinois' Accelerated Computer Science Fundamentals specialization — object-oriented programming, ordered and unordered data structures in C++ — and I document my study publicly in a GitHub repository [링크]. I am enrolled in credit-bearing coursework at UC San Diego Extended Studies [Python Programming Fundamentals, 11월 수강 시 기재], with data structures and discrete mathematics to follow. My mathematical foundation comes from engineering: linear algebra through engineering mathematics [성적표 과목명/성적 확인 후 기재], and probability sharpened daily by risk analysis on a project where I helped analyze contract risk on non-destructive examination services worth approximately $150M.
>
> My goal is specific: to become an industrial AI engineer for the energy sector. Energy infrastructure is entering a decade of AI adoption — predictive maintenance, autonomous inspection, operational optimization — and the engineers best positioned to build those systems are those who understand both the plant and the code. I offer the first half from seven years in nuclear power; the MCS provides the second. The Online MCS is the only format compatible with my life: I work full-time on an active nuclear construction site in Egypt and will relocate to Dubai in 2028, and Illinois' online delivery lets me sustain graduate study across both. Courses in [구체 과목 2~3개: e.g., Cloud Computing Applications, Distributed Systems, Applied Machine Learning] map directly onto the systems I intend to build.
>
> I have operated some of the most safety-critical control systems in the world. I am ready to learn how to build what I once only operated.

## 2. Personal Statement (초안, 약 240단어 / 250 한도)

> The control room taught me how to be calm; construction sites taught me how to be understood. My career has run through three countries and two languages of work — the precise, procedural Korean of a nuclear control room, and the negotiated English of an international EPC project in Egypt, where I conduct technical negotiations and defend engineering positions across Korean, Russian, and Egyptian teams. Moving between these worlds taught me that expertise only matters when it survives translation: across disciplines, cultures, and levels of technical fluency.
>
> That belief shapes how I work. At my previous plant, I turned my operating experience into revised procedures and internal training, and served as an in-house instructor; in Egypt, I wrote the technical guidebook my colleagues now use for a reactor technology none of us had worked with before. I automate not to replace judgment but to free it — every tool I have built returns hours to engineers for the decisions that actually need them.
>
> I bring the same instinct to my own growth. I study computer science in public, in a repository anyone can inspect, because the discipline of being seen keeps me honest. In 2028 I will relocate to Dubai, placing me in one of the fastest-growing energy-technology markets in the world. I intend to arrive not as a newcomer to software, but as an engineer who spent two years preparing — openly, consistently, and with the same rigor I once owed a reactor.
